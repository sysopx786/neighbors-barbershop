About

The story behind the chair.

105 N. Main Street, Spring City

You’re welcome to sit on the bench.

Not just if you’re getting a haircut. Not just if you have an appointment. Everyone.

Justin Kyle opened Neighbors because he wanted a place that felt like the barbershop he grew up in back in Brooklyn — somewhere kids could stop in, adults could talk, and you didn’t have to spend money to be part of it.

“You don’t have to spend money to be a part of this,” he says.

The shop is for haircuts, fades, beard work, and straight-razor shaves. It’s also for the conversations that happen while you’re in the chair, the kid who needs a water or a snack, and the people who just want to sit outside for a minute.

Justin’s been cutting hair for over 20 years. He spent time at Iron’s Edge in Phoenixville and helped open Unchained in Kimberton before opening Neighbors on Main Street. He wanted traditional barbering — clean fades, proper beard work, hot towels, straight razors — done in a way that still feels like a neighborhood spot.

Prices are kept reasonable for local families. The work is consistent. And the door is open.

We’re neighbors here. Come by. Sit on the bench. Get a cut if you need one. Stay and talk if you want.

What sets the shop apart

  • Clean fades, precise finish.
  • Consistent service, every visit.
  • Respect for your time. Appointments stay on schedule.
  • A space that feels local the moment you arrive.

The barbers

Justin Kyle

Owner & founder

With over 20 years of experience Justin has seen it all. Hailing from some of the greatest barbers in the world, he has apprenticed under barbershops that have shaped the talent he displays today. Any cut, any style. Setting trends is his forte. Forever learning, he opened this shop to bring traditional barbering back into the community he lives in.

From the block

Neighbors helping neighbors

A haircut for a kid who needs one.

Mayor Adam Alberico with Justin Kyle at the shop.

Clients left money so a school-age kid could come in for a haircut for school. The cut was covered, and so was the tip. Juan P. started it. A young man came in as Juan was leaving and got his, thanks to Juan.

Justin told the mayor a kid could walk in, say they were there for a school cut, and that was it. No questions. The point was to spend that money on school supplies, shoes, or groceries instead.

The mayor announced it for the week starting Tuesday, August 18, 2026.

If you want to sponsor the next one, stop in or call. Justin is keeping a list of the money and who gave it.

The kids’ cut on the board is still $30 unless a neighbor has already paid for that one.

“This is what community is all about.”

He stopped in, thanked the neighbors who donated, and told people they could walk in at 105 N. Main Street.

Mayor Adam Alberico, Spring City
